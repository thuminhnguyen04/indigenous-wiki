====== Understanding Seasonal Knowledge of First Nations Peoples ======

===== Introduction: Aboriginal Seasons ======
First Nations people have deep knowledge about the environment. All the seasons are mainly based on environmental changes rather than specific months. Every season marks important ecological events like plant flowering, animal behavior, and weather patterns.

===== Victoria’s Aboriginal Seasons: Wurundjeri Seasons (Kulin Nation) ======
  - **Late Summer (Biderap)**: Jan-Feb, a dry heat season when reptiles are active.
  - **Autumn (Luk)**: March, cool with mists and first rains.
  - **Winter (Waring)**: April-July, cold and wet, and possums breed.
  - **Early Spring (Guling)**: August, with lengthening days and wattling flowers.
  - **Late Spring (Poorneet)**: Sept-Oct, mild weather and eel migration.
  - **Early Summer (Ballambar)**: November, hot season with snakes in the surroundings.
  - **Garrawang**: December.

===== New South Wales: Seasons of the Yuin Nation ======
  - **Gadhu (Summer)**: Dec-Feb, hot weather with abundant food choices.
  - **Burran (Autumn)**: March-May, cooler weather with berries and fruits.
  - **Wirritjiribin (Winter)**: June-August, coldest season when wombats emerge and whales migrate.
  - **Ngoonungi (Spring)**: Sep-Nov, season of happiness with plants blossoming and warmer days.

===== South Australia: Seasons of the Nyoongar Nation ======
  - **Birak**: DEC-JAN (Summer): Hot and dry season when reptiles thrive.
  - **Bunuru**: FEB-MARCH (Autumn): Cooler weather with winds, animals prepare for winter.
  - **Djeren**: APRIL-MAY (Winter): Frogs croak, rainy and cold weather.
  - **Makuru**: JUNE-JULY (Spring): Plants and flowers bloom, bird breeding season.
  - **Djilba**: AUG-SEP: Cold and Clear.
  - **Kambarang**: OCT-NOV: Warm and wildflower season.

===== Tiwi Seasons: Melville and Bathurst Islands ======
  - **Major Tiwi Seasons**
    - **Kumunupunari**: March to August, dry season, cold weather.
    - **Jamutakari**: September – November, build-up to the wet season.
    - **Tiyari**: December – February, wet season, hot and rainy.
  - **Minor Tiwi Overlapping Seasons**
    - **Wurringawunari**: Season of the knock-em-downs; dry winds flatten tall grass.
    - **Kimirrakinari**: Season of fire; dry grass is burnt.
    - **Pumutingari**: Season of the wind that flakes skin.
    - **Yirriwinari and Mirniputari**: Cold weather in mid-June to July, signalled by flowering of Wurritjinga (Eucalyptus confertiflora).
    - **Kumwari**: Season of fog; low temperatures and morning fog.
    - **Yartupwari**: Season of the dry creek bed; waterholes dry up.
    - **Milikitorinari**: Season of hot feet; food gathering in mangroves and jungle patches.
    - **Pumwanyingar**: Season of thunder; increased humidity but little rain.
    - **Kurukurari**: Season of the mangrove worm; worms are easy to find.
    - **Mumpikari**: Season of muddy possum tracks; first rains signal possums returning to trees.
    - **Tawutawungari**: Season of the clap sticks; Kurlama ceremonies held.
    - **Wurrijingari**: Season of the flowers.

For more details, visit [CSIRO](https://www.csiro.au).

===== Miriwoong Seasons: Kununurra, Western Australia ======
  - **Warnkamarn**: May – July, cold season.
  - **Yinengaleng**: August – October, hot and dry.
  - **Gurrung**: November – December, hot season.
  - **Mantha**: January – April, wet season.

===== Gooniyandi and Walmajarri Seasons: Western Australia ======
  - **Gooniyandi Seasons**
    - **Barranga**: April-June, very hot weather time.
    - **Yidirla**: July-Sep, wet season time when the river runs.
    - **Ngamari**: Oct-Dec, female cold weather time.
    - **Girlinggoowa**: Jan-April, male cold weather time.
  - **Walmajarri Seasons**
    - **Parranga**: Dec-Feb, hot weather time.
    - **Yitilal**: March-June, raining time.
    - **Makurra**: July-Oct, cold weather time.

===== Kunwinjku Seasons: Northern Territory ======
  - **Kudjewk**: Jan-Mar, the monsoon time with heavy rains brings kudjarr (flooding and flowing water) and kunmayorrk (strong winds). It’s a time of abundant fruits with the Yukkuyyukku (firefly lights) flashing at night to signal the bush fruits are ready to collect.
  - **Bangkerreng**: April, Nakurl (knock ’em down storms) coming from koyek (the east) flattens Manbedje yirridjdja (the early spear grass). Manbedje duwa (the later spear grass) is still green and growing until barra (the later rains). It is the time for collecting magpie goose eggs, Manimunak wirlarrk and a good time for fishing.
  - **Yekke**: May-June, the rains have finished. The cooler temperature and the wind blowing from north east to west signals the start of the dry time. Lots of Djalangkarridjdjalangkarridj (dragonflies) and Manbarndarr (Calytrix exstipulata) and Mandjedj (Cochlospermum fraseri) flowering are a sign of Yekke.
  - **Wurrkeng**: July-August, Kunbonjdjek- the cool weather time, when dry winds blow from the east, koyek kunmayorrk. Many plants are flowering, like Mandjalen (Eucalyptus miniata), Manbordokorr (Eucalyptus tetrodonta) and Mandadjek (Grevillea pteridifolia) and the native bees are busy so it’s a good time to collect Mankung, sugar bag.
  - **Kurrung**: Sep-Oct, the hot, dry time where the air is fragrant from many plants flowering, like Manboyberre (Syzgium forte) and Mandjarduk (Syzgium suborbiculare). It’s a very good time for hunting waterbirds like Manimunak (magpie goose), Ngalmangiyi (northern long-necked turtle) with kawurluwurlhme kunak (floodplain burning) to clear the kundalk (grass) as well as hunt Kedjebe (Arafura filesnake) around the edge of billabongs.
  - **Kunumeleng**: Nov-Dec, the time of kangolmarnburren, when the humidity builds, kunngol (clouds) and kunmayorrk (wind) gather together from all directions. Animals hear kangurdulme (thunder) and know it’s time for a change. Namarrkon kamayhmayhke (lightning flashes) tell us that the Ngalmangiyi and Kedjebe are moving closer to the bank and are easier to collect.

===== Cultural and Environmental Significance ======
There is a deep connection between people, land, and animals. Knowledge about the seasons has been passed down through generations. It helps manage natural resources, predict weather, and sustain biodiversity.
