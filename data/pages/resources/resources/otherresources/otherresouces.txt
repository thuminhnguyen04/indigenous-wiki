| {{:8_ways.jpg?360|}} \\ **8 Ways ** \\ This website explains the ‘8 Ways’ approach, which is an Aboriginal method of teaching and learning. Instead of focusing just on books and lectures, Aboriginal teaching values ways like visual learning, telling stories, and connecting with nature. For example, this website teaches us how storytelling can be a powerful way to share knowledge. In an Aboriginal classroom, a teacher might use a story to teach a lesson, rather than just giving instructions. This approach is very hands-on and helps us understand learning from an Aboriginal perspective \\ \\ [[https://www.8ways.online|Visit Resource]] | {{:abm01.jpg?360|}} \\ **Indigenous Weather Knowledge** \\ This website is run by Australia’s Bureau of Meteorology and shares weather knowledge from Aboriginal communities. Aboriginal people have always had a deep understanding of the weather and the environment. Unlike the typical four seasons, Aboriginal groups in Australia recognize many different seasons based on natural signs. For example, the Noongar people in Western Australia recognize six seasons, which are based on changes in the plants, animals, and weather around them. This site lets us learn how different Aboriginal groups read nature to understand the world around them. \\ \\ [[http://www.bom.gov.au/iwk/|Visit Resource]] | 
| {{:cg.png?360|}} \\ **Common Ground ** \\ Common Ground is a fantastic website that helps people learn about Aboriginal stories, symbols, and history. In Aboriginal culture, symbols are often used in art and have special meanings. For example, a U shape can represent a person sitting. This website also shares Aboriginal stories and histories that aren’t usually taught in regular history classes, helping us learn about Australia’s past from an Aboriginal viewpoint. Common Ground helps us understand the meanings behind symbols and shows us why stories are important for keeping culture alive. \\ \\ [[https://www.commonground.org.au/|Visit Resource]] | {{:art2.jpg?360|}} \\ **Aboriginal Art Australia** \\ Aboriginal Art Australia focuses on Indigenous art. Aboriginal art is famous around the world and is unique because it’s tied to the land, stories, and spirituality. This website explains different art styles like dot painting and rock art, and it shows us how each artwork tells a story about culture or history. For example, an artist like Emily Kame Kngwarreye created beautiful paintings that represented her deep connection to the land. This website helps us see art not just as decoration but as a way of expressing cultural knowledge. \\ \\ [[https://www.aboriginal-art-australia.com/aboriginal-art-library/aboriginal-art-styles/|Visit Resource]]  |
| {{:art1.jpg?360|}} \\ **Dreamtime** \\ The Dreamtime website is all about Aboriginal creation stories, which explain how the land, animals, and people came to be. These stories are passed down from generation to generation and teach important lessons. For example, the story of the Rainbow Serpent is a well-known Dreamtime story that teaches respect for nature and each other. By reading these stories, we learn about the beliefs and values of Aboriginal people, and we see how important storytelling is in keeping traditions alive. \\ \\ [[https://dreamtime.net.au|Visit Resource]]  | {{:art2.jpg?360|}} \\ **AIATSIS - Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies** \\ AIATSIS, or the Australian Institute of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Studies, is a big organization dedicated to protecting and sharing Aboriginal culture and knowledge. They have an enormous library filled with photos, documents, recordings, and other historical materials about Aboriginal people. They even help families trace their ancestry and learn about their history. One of their well-known resources is a map that shows different Aboriginal language groups across Australia, helping us understand just how diverse these communities are. \\ \\ [[https://aiatsis.gov.au//|Visit Resource]]   | 
| {{:art1.jpg?360|}} \\ **Australians Together** \\ Australians Together is a website made for learning about Aboriginal and non-Aboriginal relations in Australia. It provides resources for schools to help students understand the history and culture of Aboriginal people. This website focuses a lot on reconciliation, which is about building positive relationships between Aboriginal and non-Aboriginal Australians. For example, it has videos and activities that teach students about Australia’s history and how we can work together toward a more united future. \\ \\ [[https://australianstogether.org.au|Visit Resource]]  | {{:art2.jpg?360|}} \\ **ABC Education - Indigenous News & Resources** \\ ABC Education is part of ABC’s educational section and provides news related to aboriginal people and also provides a lot of resources for learning about Aboriginal histories and cultures. They have videos, lesson plans, and articles designed for schools. For instance, they have videos explaining important topics like the Uluru Statement, a statement calling for Indigenous rights. ABC Education helps students understand Aboriginal culture and history in a simple and engaging way.\\ \\ [[https://www.abc.net.au/indigenous|Visit Resource]]| 
| {{:art1.jpg?360|}} \\ **Reconciliation Australia** \\ Reconciliation Australia is an organization that works to bring Aboriginal and non-Aboriginal Australians closer together. They provide something called Reconciliation Action Plans, or RAPs, which are guides that schools, businesses, and communities can follow to improve relationships and understanding. For example, they might help a workplace create policies that respect Aboriginal traditions. Reconciliation Australia is all about promoting respect and understanding, helping people from all backgrounds come together. \\ \\  [[https://www.reconciliation.org.au|Visit Resource]] | {{:art2.jpg?360|}} \\ **First Languages Australia** \\ First Languages Australia is a site dedicated to preserving and teaching Aboriginal languages. Many Aboriginal languages are at risk of disappearing, so this organization works hard to keep them alive. They have a project called 'Our Languages,' which maps where each Aboriginal language is spoken. This is very important because language is a key part of identity and culture. By learning Aboriginal languages, people can connect with the land and the culture in a deeper way.\\ \\ [[https://www.firstlanguages.org.au/|Visit Resource]] | 
| {{:art1.jpg?360|}} \\ **Australian Museum – First Nations Collection** \\ The Australian Museum website’s First Nations section provides information about Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories, cultures, and knowledge. It’s a place to learn about the deep connection Aboriginal people have with the land, as well as their traditions, beliefs, and contributions. This site explores topics like kinship (relationships within families and communities), sacred sites, and environmental knowledge. The "Unsettled" exhibition offers resources that share stories of Australia’s history from Indigenous perspectives, covering topics such as colonization and resistance. The website also has sections on the importance of land, totems, and traditional art.\\ \\ [[https://australian.museum/learn/first-nations|Visit Resource]]   | {{:art2.jpg?360|}} \\ **Australian Curriculum – Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures** \\The Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures section of the Australian Curriculum focuses on integrating Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures into school subjects. It provides resources and guidance for teachers to include Indigenous perspectives in all areas of learning. It covers three main themes – Country/Place, Culture, and People – helping students understand Indigenous ways of knowing and their contributions to knowledge. The curriculum includes activities that connect science, like studying native plants used by Aboriginal people for food and medicine, and history lessons on Aboriginal leaders. It supports educators in teaching a well-rounded view of Australian history.\\ \\ [[https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/resources/aboriginal-and-torres-strait-islander-histories-and-culture|Visit Resource]] |
